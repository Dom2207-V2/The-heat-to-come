## Purpose and experience

The Heat to Come is a browser-based climate-adaptation decision game set on a campus. Players join a Task Force, allocate limited resources across three turns, face changing conditions and examine the consequences of their decisions. The subtitle is **A Climate Simulator Game**. The campus situations are teaching scenarios, not assessments of named real institutions.

The central activity is to explain a sequence of choices. A portfolio that appears attractive at one moment may create delivery demands, delayed benefits or social compromises later. The game makes those relationships visible through intervention cards, a campus map, event explanations and a final debrief. It provides no single prescribed solution.

The interface uses British English. It runs without an account and keeps run data locally unless a user exports it. A classroom can therefore use the game without a central student database. Distribution packaging and online publication are separate stages: this dossier accompanies the local candidate and does not establish that a 2.5.0 website or Windows package has been published.

## What learners should practise

These are intended learning objectives, not demonstrated educational outcomes. A facilitated session can ask learners to:

1. Distinguish current climate risk from the capacity to withstand and respond to future disruption.
2. Explain why a financially affordable package can still be difficult to coordinate or deliver.
3. Consider timing: immediate relief, delayed activation, maturing capability and the consequences of committing resources early.
4. Explain a trade-off between protection, continuity, acceptability and flexibility using the recorded trajectory.
5. Revise a plan in response to information that has actually been revealed, without assuming knowledge of future events.
6. Compare alternatives under reproducible conditions and distinguish an observed difference from a general causal claim.
7. Critique the limits of a simplified model before transferring an insight to a real campus.

## Four situations, shared decision tools

| Scenario | Starting tension | Discussion focus |
|---|---|---|
| The Pressure Cooker | High visible risk with more resources | Urgency, delivery and justified commitment |
| The Fragile Frontline | High risk with limited resources and low resilience | Prioritisation and implementability under scarcity |
| The Comfort Zone | Lower current risk with stronger starting resilience | Proportionality and preserving future options |
| The Silent Risk | Lower visible risk with weak starting resilience | Recognising latent vulnerability and preparing early |

All four scenarios use the same intervention catalogue. Their current numeric conditions and release schedules belong to the scenario reference in this dossier. Original V1 scenario documents preserve the conceptual lineage; their historic budgets and calibration values must not be used as the current rules.

## What the model represents

Risk, resilience, continuity and acceptability are synthetic indices. Budget units are relative game resources, not a currency. Interventions represent simplified adaptation options with authored costs, delivery timing, interactions and effects. Events are designed stresses and opportunities governed by reproducible random draws. A replay requires the same build, scenario, seed and choices to reproduce the full result.

The score summarises a simulated trajectory for reflection. It is not a learner grade, a climate forecast, a probability of safety, a percentage of an optimal real-world plan or an investment appraisal. The workbook accompanying the dossier has its own declared reproduction scope; it must not be assumed to be a second complete game engine.

## Teaching use and evidence boundary

Individual exploration, small-group deliberation and paired replay are suggested uses. The facilitator guide supplies a practical session structure and a way to collect observations without equating scores with understanding. These suggestions have not been validated in a classroom study.

The G1 run03 handoff records technical qualification of the corrected software, including the exact scope of automated games, replay and data exchange. That evidence does not establish learning gains, enjoyment, native-speaker review, universal accessibility or physical mobile-device support. The historical archive also has a specifically accepted loss recorded privately in the G1 handoff; the current dossier does not claim that all earlier releases can be reconstructed.
